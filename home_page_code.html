Mission, Vision & Values

rocket_launch

Mission

To teach students in Shamva the foundations of AI engineering in a way that is accessible, practical, and directly connected to the problems they see in their own lives — equipping them not just with technical skill, but with the confidence to apply it to their community.

visibility

Vision

An independent, self-sustaining organization based in Shamva that continues to grow long after the first cohort graduates — with a permanent club at Wadzanai High School, graduates mentoring new students, and a community that no longer sees problem-solving as something reserved for adults or for elsewhere.

favorite

Values

Curiosity over memorization. Building over lecturing. Real problems over abstract exercises. We believe students learn fastest when what they build matters to someone — starting with themselves, and extending to their families, their school, and their community.

The Problem & Our Solution

The Problem

Students in Shamva are deeply curious and capable, but they've had almost no exposure to computer science, programming, or modern technology. Of the students surveyed at Wadzanai High School before our first program, not a single one knew any programming language, and only 13.7% had even heard of AI. Schools don't teach robotics, internet access is limited, and the most visible economic path is small-scale gold mining. Meanwhile, the community faces real and urgent challenges — drug abuse, early child marriage, water and electricity shortages, high unemployment — and young people currently have few tools to respond to any of it.

Our Solution

A six-week, live-taught program that takes students from their very first line of code to a fully built, deployed AI-powered application — one they design themselves, around a problem they've identified in their own community. Two Zoom sessions a week plus a hands-on lab keep learning consistent and practical, while a trusted partner on the ground ensures students have the space, equipment, and support to make it real. By the final week, every student has something they can point to and say: I made that, and you can use it.
