About the program

How the program began

Shamva Innovators grew from a small course held during the December 2023 school holiday. Six students from Shamva spent three weeks learning the basics of C, C++, and electronics. They shared one borrowed laptop and two borrowed electronics kits, and they used mobile phones to write code. Before the course, 51 students at Wadzanai High School were surveyed. None knew a programming language.

The current program teaches the foundations of AI engineering through problems students see in Shamva, including drug abuse, water and electricity shortages, early child marriage, and unemployment. The long-term goal is an independent, locally led organization where graduates mentor new students and young people build software for their community.

Students work from a dedicated room at Wadzanai High School. Each week, they attend two live Zoom lessons and one hands-on lab session. Over six weeks, they learn Python, build a web application that uses AI, publish it online, and share it with other people.

Why the program exists

For many young people in Shamva, small-scale gold mining is the most visible way to earn a living. Local schools do not teach robotics. Computer science is part of the curriculum, but students get little practice in computational thinking, problem-solving, or building software for everyday use. Limited internet access also makes it difficult for them to learn about AI.

Learning Python and AI gives students another set of skills to use at work, in further study, and in their own projects. The course also asks them to apply those skills close to home. A student might build a tool about drug-abuse prevention, access to medicine, or helping girls stay in school. The subject of each project comes from a problem the student has identified.

How the course works

school

Learn

The course runs for six weeks and covers Python, Streamlit, and AI APIs. Students attend two live Zoom lessons and one hands-on lab each week.

build

Build

Students practice each lesson by writing software. Their projects address problems they have identified in their community, such as drug-abuse awareness, early warning systems, and access to information.

rocket_launch

Publish

In the final week, every student publishes a working application online and presents it at a community showcase. Anyone with the link can open and use the project.

Six weeks from start to finish

Weeks 1–2

Python basics

Students learn variables, conditions, loops, and functions. They practice breaking a problem into steps and writing code for each step.

Week 3

Web applications with Streamlit

Students use Streamlit to give their Python code a simple web interface. They can then share what they have built with other people.

Weeks 4–5

AI models and APIs

Students learn the basic ideas behind AI models. They use APIs to connect a model to their own application and add a useful AI feature.

Week 6

Final projects

Students complete their final projects, publish them online, and present them at a community showcase.
